Why your photo keeps getting rejected on application portals

Two kilobytes too big, ten pixels too wide, or a signature photographed in bad light.

You have the qualification. You have paid the fee. You are on the last page of the form, and the portal says the photograph could not be uploaded. No reason given, or a reason that makes no sense.

It is almost always one of five things.

The file is a few kilobytes too big

Portals set a maximum, often somewhere between 20 and 100 KB for a photograph. A picture straight from a phone camera is two to four megabytes — fifty times too large. Resizing the dimensions is not the same as reducing the file size, which is why people shrink the photo and still get rejected.

Some portals also set a minimum. A photo squashed down to 3 KB gets refused for being too small just as surely as an oversized one. If you have compressed a picture again and again until it finally uploaded, you may have gone past the floor without realising.

The dimensions are wrong

Common sizes asked for are around 200 × 230 pixels for a photograph and around 140 × 60 for a signature, but they vary between recruitments. The notification states the size. Use the number in the notification, not one a friend sent you on WhatsApp for a different exam last year.

It went through WhatsApp

This is the quiet one. When you send a photo on WhatsApp, it is compressed before it arrives. The copy your cousin sent you looks fine on screen and is useless for a form — the detail has already been thrown away, so when the portal asks for a sharper file there is nothing left to work with.

Always start from the original picture, taken on the camera, not a copy that has been forwarded.

The background

Most government forms want a plain light background. A photograph taken against a patterned wall, a door, or a crowd will often be rejected by whoever checks it later, even if the portal accepts the upload.

Stand a metre or so in front of a plain white or light wall, face the camera straight on, in daylight but not direct sun. No cap, no sunglasses, no filter.

The signature

Sign on white, unlined paper with a black or blue pen. Photograph it from directly above, in daylight — not at an angle, and not with your own shadow falling across it.

A signature scanned from a lined notebook page, with the lines visible, gets refused often enough to be worth avoiding.

One last thing

Do this part the day before, not ten minutes before the deadline. Portals get slow on the final day, and the upload step is where people lose a form they had otherwise finished.

Save the finished photo and signature somewhere you can find them again. You will need them for the next application too.
